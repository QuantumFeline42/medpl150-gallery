Emotive Eye Animated

With the starting point of the starstruck eye I wanted to add more animation than just the blinking of the eyelids, and the obvious element to use for that was the stars reflecting in the iris. I experimented with a couple ways to give the stars animation, either by making them grow/shrink, or making them fade out and reappear, before settling on making them spin around. While trying different rotation speeds I ran into an issue where since the element was flat color and symmetrical if I made them rotate certain numbers of degrees each frame then it wouldn’t look like it was rotating much at all as the points wouldn’t vary much frame by frame. With some experimenting I was able to find a rotation rate using a couple different degrees so it felt like it was spinning, and also added a bit of backwards rotation at the beginning to use the anticipation animation trick.

Without the rest of the face or an eyebrow to specify the emotion it could have been one of a number of them like shock or fear or curiosity, so I thought about some ways that I could narrow it down. Thinking back to anime I remembered how they will often turn the pupil into a star to denote being ‘starstruck’ with intense admiration or interest, but instead of doing that specifically I decided to decorate the iris with stars like they’re reflected off the surface.

For the blinking itself it proved to be too tricky and busy to properly reflect the separate eyelashes that were suggested in the original still drawing through a dotted line, so I removed those to give the eyelid a cleaner border while in motion. To give the act of closing a bit of extra punch I also added in another star that appears and bounces out of the eye. If it were given audio it’s easy to imagine a clear ringing sound accompanying the wink.

For the background I didn’t want it to distract too much from the main eye so while I tested making the background stars spin or have larger stars shoot by in the foreground I settled on splitting the group of stars into three smaller groups, each fading then reappearing to give the impression of subtly twinkling.
