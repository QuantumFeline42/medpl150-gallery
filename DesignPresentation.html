Design Presentation

When I was thinking of subjects to potentially use for the puppet animation, it didn’t take long for me to notice some of my articulated animated figurines near my home computer. They come from a popular line of Japanese figurines admired for their range of motion and how many types of poses they can be put into. While I’ve seen figurines from the same line used for actual stop motion animation it made sense that they could be a good base image for a Photoshop puppet animation as, unlike solid molded figurines, I would be able to get a photo of the figure in a T-Pose to get all the various body parts at correct proportions and viewed head on.

I chose the figure of Chilchuck, a rogue from a fantasy adventure anime, because his outfit didn’t obstruct his limbs so they would read well in an animation. He even came with different hands, faces, and items I could use. Being a rogue I had a lot of ideas for what he could be doing, such as picking the lock on a chest or sneaking around, but given that I haven’t done this style of animation before I decided to do something that would work well in a short, repeating animation while still making use of some of the extra bits I had available.

Flipping a coin felt like a good motion that could involve motion of the arm and the upper body, and I added in a bit of motion to the opposite leg to balance it out. I took several photos of the coin from different angles to use as it flipped, and the hands provided worked surprisingly well to swap between and give a good approximation of both the initial flick and the following catch.

While the coin was in the air I realized that the character froze up as they waited for the coin to fall again, so I had the idea to manipulate the eyes in Photoshop to follow the coin. I’m pretty proud of the result and that it ends where it started for a consistent looping gif.
