A Reflection on 'Your Brain on ChatGPT'

The "Your Brain on ChatGPT" article was an interesting study that helped take something that a lot of us are suspecting is the case (that using LLM programs is diminishing out mental processes) and gave it more of a scientific backing. While the scoring of the various essays has more of a subjective judgment, the self-reported feelings of a lack of ownership and their inability to even remember much of what they'd just submitted are much more telling, while the EEG readings are most worrisome of all. It's hard not to walk away with the impression that the mental skills, like our muscles, also fall into the category of 'use it or lose it.' Key skills that used to be very important to excelling in life can now easily be delegated to machines that can perform them far faster and in large quantities, and, at the moment, there is not sufficient disincentive to do so. On the contrary, corporations and governments are pushing for it, as they see it as a route to greater productivity and profit.

The round table discussion was fascinating in that while there was a clear stance that the current implementation of AI is overall harmful, the host and panelists were not solidly against it. The host especially had conflicting thoughts about AI's usefulness, and it's something I've seen elsewhere in the content creator space. Most notably with Hank Green, a YouTube science communicator who recently got caught increasingly relying on LLM programs to be more productive until eventually the 'voice' of AI began to creep into his content in a way that his fans noticed and called him out on. The pressure to churn out content for the algorithm and audience pushes people toward tools like AI, especially if others in the same space are using it and you risk falling behind.

It's a depressing form of short term, hustle culture thinking that, in the end, only really benefits those at the top who will make the most profit while cutting out as much labor as they can and expecting those left working to use AI tools to be more and more productive. If something can be done quicker then that's how it should be done, even if it reduces the human element to just pushing a button to get the AI to generate something that gets a quick once-over before being approved. It's easy to see how that becomes progressively more harmful to the brain.

The part about struggle being good for the brain brought to mind something I've thought about a lot lately in regards to hobbies and other pursuits. Humans naturally enjoy doing things themselves. I believe it triggers parts of the brain that evolved to make us feel like we're making ourselves safer and more secure in our basic needs, but the modern world does too much to separate the actions we undertake for our jobs from the fruits of that labor in our own lives, and as a result of course we would want to find ways to do that quicker and easier. But I believe most of us do still want to learn, create, build, and solve problems ourselves, and I hope AI doesn't take all of that away from us.
