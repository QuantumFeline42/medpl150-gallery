A Reflection on 'Motherhood in Crisis' by Al Jazeera

The graphic novel I chose for the assignment was Kadiatu: The Devil at My Door. As a story about medical procedures with scenes that involved incredibly sick children the use of photographs might have been too disturbing for some readers and could have turned them off from engaging with the story. In addition, since much of the story is told about the woman’s past in an incredibly poor part of the world, photographs would be rare compared to first world nations where one would have a more complete photo record of their past. Taking a person’s narrated story and expanding it with illustrations also makes it more evocative without the person themselves having to be more descriptive in a way that would change their natural way of speaking as one would in prose to convey more details.

While most of the backgrounds in the comic section were hand-drawn, there were some notable instances of photographs used as backgrounds with illustrated people drawn into them. They were effective in grounding the narrative in reality a bit before the big change over to entirely photographic. The choice of transitions to make that switch was effective, bridging the gap from the woman’s tragic past having dealt with the superstitions and judgments around her into the present where she has her healthy and happy children with her. The first photograph depicting both children smiling in an open doorway is very welcoming, a depiction of the woman coming home. It’s also a contrast to the title of the work, where before her lost children were blamed on evil and possession, instead of a devil at her door it’s joy and innocence.

There is a lot more brightness in the photos compared to the choice to use more somber and muted colors in the illustrations, particularly in the backgrounds which are often dark and shadowy even during the daytime. The aspect ratio changes in the transition from comic illustration to photographs as well. While a comic it sticks to a more traditional portrait orientation which can feel cramped and claustrophobic when surrounded on both sides of the web page by black, the imagery then opens up into full width photographs giving the sense of a life with more openness, freedom, and possibility, as well as pushing back the blackness of the page to produce more light through the monitor.

The final shift from photographs to video where a doctor explains the treatment given to Abdul creates an additional step in technology similar to the choice to finally trust more modern medicine as opposed to the traditional healers of her village who blamed the prior deaths on possession.

Telling a non-fiction human interest story through comics isn’t done as much as articles with text and photographs. While a text article can present more comprehensive information and accompanying photographs capture specific moments in time, the art form of a comic allows for telling a more complete narrative with moments that visually stick into memory more easily without requiring a large budget if they had wanted to use video footage.
